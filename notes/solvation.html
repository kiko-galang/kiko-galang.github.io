Part I: Derivation of the Average Solvation Number

Motivation. Solvation free energy reflects the cost of surrounding a charged species with a shell of oriented solvent molecules — specifically, the entropy decrease due to free solvent molecules binding to ions and limiting accessible configurations. We follow a thermodynamic route to relate the average solvation number and binding equilibria to chemical potentials and activity coefficients, naturally bridging molecular-scale solvation with macroscopic observables.

Step 1: Foundational Definitions and Mass Balances

The Average Solvation Number

We define the average solvation number (number of bound solvent molecules per cation) as:

\[ \bar{h} = \frac{\text{total bound solvent molecules}}{\text{total cations}} = \frac{n_{\mathrm{H_2O}}^b}{n_C^t} \]

where \(n_{\mathrm{H_2O}}^b\) is the total number of bound water molecules and \(n_C^t\) is the total number of cations.

Mass Balance of Species

Define:

  • \(n_{\mathrm{H_2O}}^t\): total water molecules
  • \(n_{\mathrm{H_2O}}^f\): free (unbound) water molecules
  • \(n_{C_i}\): cations with \(i\) bound water molecules
  • \(N\): maximum solvation sites per cation

From mass balance:

\[ n_{\mathrm{H_2O}}^t = n_{\mathrm{H_2O}}^f + \sum_{i=0}^N i\, n_{C_i} \qquad n_{\mathrm{H_2O}}^b = \sum_{i=0}^N i\, n_{C_i} \qquad n_C^t = \sum_{i=0}^N n_{C_i} \]

Thus:

\[ \bar{h} = \frac{\sum_{i=0}^N i\, n_{C_i}}{\sum_{i=0}^N n_{C_i}} = \frac{\sum_{i=0}^N i\, x_i}{\sum_{i=0}^N x_i} \]

where \(x_i = n_{C_i}/n_C^t\) is the mole fraction of cations with \(i\) bound water molecules.

Step 2: Microscopic and Macroscopic Equilibria

Distinguishing Equilibrium Constants (\(k\) vs \(K\))

It is crucial to distinguish between the macroscopic constant \(K_i\), which describes the overall formation of a species with \(i\) bound molecules, and the microscopic constant \(k\), which describes the intrinsic affinity for any single specific binding site. Macroscopic constants are influenced by combinatorics; the microscopic constant is not.

The macroscopic equilibrium constant \(K_1\) relates to all species with one bound solvent molecule regardless of which site is occupied:

\[ K_1 = \frac{[C_1]}{[C_0][\mathrm{H_2O}]} \]

In contrast, microscopic constants \(k_i\) refer to binding at specific individual sites. The macroscopic constants relate to populations summed over indistinguishable microstates, while microscopic constants describe specific binding site interactions.

Combinatorics of Binding Sites

For cations with \(N\) identical binding sites and \(i\) bound waters, the number of unique microstates is:

\[ \Omega_{N,i} = \binom{N}{i} = \frac{N!}{i!(N-i)!} \]

For model simplicity, we assume all sites bind identically, so \(k_i = k_j\), though \(K_i \neq K_j\) due to combinatorics.

Step 3: Deriving a Closed-Form Expression for \(\bar{h}\)

Define equilibrium constants based on activities. In general:

\[ K_i = \frac{a_i}{a_{i-1}\, a_w} \implies a_i = a_0\, a_w^i \prod_{j=1}^i K_j \]

Assuming \(a_i \approx x_i\):

\[ x_i = x_0\, x_w^i \prod_{j=1}^i K_j \]

Relating Macroscopic and Microscopic Constants

For the general equilibrium \(C_{i-1} + \mathrm{H_2O} \rightleftharpoons C_i\), normalizing concentrations by the number of corresponding microstates:

\[ k = \frac{\left(\frac{[C_i]}{\Omega_{N,i}}\right)} {\left(\frac{[C_{i-1}]}{\Omega_{N,i-1}}\right)[\mathrm{H_2O}]} \implies K_i = \frac{\Omega_{N,i}}{\Omega_{N,i-1}}\, k \]

Using the binomial forms:

\[ \frac{\Omega_{N,i}}{\Omega_{N,i-1}} = \frac{N-i+1}{i} \implies \boxed{K_i = \frac{N-i+1}{i}\, k} \]

Deriving the Mole Fractions

\[ \prod_{j=1}^i K_j = \prod_{j=1}^i \frac{N-j+1}{j}\cdot k^i = \binom{N}{i} k^i \]

Therefore:

\[ x_i = x_0\,(k x_w)^i \binom{N}{i} \]

Substituting into the solvation number:

\[ \bar{h} = \frac{\sum_{i=0}^N i\, x_0 (kx_w)^i \binom{N}{i}} {\sum_{i=0}^N x_0 (kx_w)^i \binom{N}{i}} = \frac{\sum_{i=0}^N i\,(kx_w)^i \binom{N}{i}} {\sum_{i=0}^N (kx_w)^i \binom{N}{i}} \]

Evaluating the Binomial Sums

Recall the binomial identity and its derivative with respect to \(x\), multiplied by \(x\):

\[ \sum_{i=0}^N \binom{N}{i} x^i = (1+x)^N \qquad \sum_{i=0}^N i\binom{N}{i} x^i = Nx(1+x)^{N-1} \]

Let \(y = kx_w\):

\[ \bar{h} = \frac{Ny(1+y)^{N-1}}{(1+y)^N} = \frac{Ny}{1+y} \]

Substituting back:

\[ \boxed{\bar{h} = \frac{N k x_w}{1 + k x_w}} \]

This compact expression links the average number of bound solvent molecules per cation to the microscopic binding constant \(k\), the number of solvation sites \(N\), and the free water mole fraction \(x_w\).

Part II: Derivation of the Solvation Activity Coefficient

Motivation. Having established a molecular model for \(\bar{h}\), the next step is to quantify its thermodynamic effect. Binding of solvent molecules depletes the free solvent available for mixing, altering the chemical potential of all species. This section connects the microscopic solvation model to macroscopic activity coefficients.

Step 4: Connecting Solvation to Gibbs Free Energy

Assume complete dissociation \(E \to \nu_C C + \nu_A A\). The chemical potential of the electrolyte is:

\[ \mu_E = \nu_C \mu_C + \nu_A \mu_A \]

The total Gibbs free energy is \(G = n_0\mu_0 + n_C\mu_C + n_A\mu_A\), and the excess Gibbs energy is:

\[ G^E = G - G^{\mathrm{ideal}} \implies \frac{G^E}{RT} = n_0 \ln\gamma_0^{\mathrm{slv}} + n_C \ln\gamma_C^{\mathrm{slv}} + n_A \ln\gamma_A^{\mathrm{slv}} \]

The chemical equilibrium condition requires:

\[ \mu_{ci} = \mu_{c0} + i\mu_0^f \]

Applying mass balances reduces the chemical Gibbs energy to \(G^{\mathrm{chem}} = n_0\mu_0^f + n_C\mu_{c0} + n_A\mu_{a0}\), showing by comparison that \(\mu_0^f = \mu_0\), \(\mu_{c0} = \mu_C\), \(\mu_{a0} = \mu_A\).

Defining Activity Coefficients from Chemical Potential

For the solvent, equating the free and apparent chemical potentials gives:

\[ \boxed{\ln\gamma_0^{\mathrm{slv}} = \ln\!\left(\frac{x_0^{\mathrm{slv}}}{x_0}\right) = \ln\alpha_0} \]

For the cation, using the infinite dilution normalization \(\gamma_C^{\mathrm{slv}} \to 1\) as \(x_C \to 0\):

\[ \boxed{\ln\gamma_C^{\mathrm{slv}} = \ln\!\left(\frac{\alpha_i}{\alpha_i^\infty}\right)} \]

Step 5: Solvent Depletion and Alpha Corrections

Solvent Correction Factor \(\alpha_0\)

\[ \alpha_0 = \frac{x_w^{\mathrm{slv}}}{x_w} \qquad\text{where}\qquad x_w^{\mathrm{slv}} = \frac{n_w - \sum_i \bar{h}_i n_i}{n_w + \sum_i n_i(1-\bar{h}_i)} \]

Therefore:

\[ \boxed{\alpha_0 = \gamma_0^{\mathrm{slv}} = \frac{1 - \sum_i \bar{h}_i\, \dfrac{n_i}{n_w}} {x_w + \sum_i x_i(1 - \bar{h}_i)}} \]

Solute Correction Factor \(\alpha_i\)

Using \(n_i^{\mathrm{slv}} = n_0(1+kx_w)^N\) and rearranging:

\[ \alpha_i = \frac{n_t}{n_t^{\mathrm{slv}}}\cdot\frac{1}{(1+kx_w)^N} \qquad \alpha_i^\infty = \lim_{x_w\to 1}\alpha_i = \frac{1}{(1+k)^N} \]

Final Expression for Solvating Species (Cations)

\[ \boxed{\gamma_i = \frac{\alpha_i}{\alpha_i^\infty} = \frac{(1+k_i)^{N_i}}{x_w\,(1+k_i x_w)^{N_i} \left(1 + \dfrac{1}{n_w}\displaystyle\sum_j n_j(1-\bar{h}_j)\right)}} \]

Final Expression for Non-Solvating Species (Anions, \(k_i = 0\))

\[ \boxed{\gamma_i = \frac{1}{x_w\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{n_w}\displaystyle\sum_j n_j(1-\bar{h}_j)\right)}} \]

Interpretation: Even though the anion does not bind water directly, its activity coefficient still reflects the global scarcity of free water — arising from competition by hydrated cations reducing available solvent for entropy of mixing. In the dilute limit \(x_w \to 1\), \(n_j \to 0\), this recovers \(\gamma_i \to 1\) as expected.

Summary

Key Assumptions

  1. Selective solvation — cations solvate; anions have \(k = 0\)
  2. Identical independent sites — all \(N\) sites on a cation bind with the same microscopic constant \(k\)
  3. Activity–concentration equivalence — \(a_i \approx x_i\) in the equilibrium expressions

Final Expressions

Quantity Expression
Average solvation number \(\bar{h}_i\) \(\dfrac{N_i k_i x_w}{1 + k_i x_w}\)
Solvent activity coefficient \(\gamma_0\) \(\dfrac{1 - \sum_i \bar{h}_i n_i/n_w}{x_w + \sum_i x_i(1-\bar{h}_i)}\)
Cation activity coefficient \(\gamma_i\) \(\dfrac{(1+k_i)^{N_i}}{x_w(1+k_i x_w)^{N_i}(1+\sum_j \frac{n_j}{n_w}(1-\bar{h}_j))}\)
Anion activity coefficient \(\gamma_i\) \(\dfrac{1}{x_w(1+\sum_j \frac{n_j}{n_w}(1-\bar{h}_j))}\)

Alpha Correction Factors

\[ \alpha_0 = \frac{x_w^{\mathrm{slv}}}{x_w} \qquad \alpha_i = \frac{n_t}{n_t^{\mathrm{slv}}}\cdot\frac{1}{(1+k_i x_w)^{N_i}} \qquad \alpha_i^\infty = \frac{1}{(1+k_i)^{N_i}} \]

The non-ideal solvation contribution to the chemical potential is directly related to these:

\[ \frac{G^E_{\mathrm{slv}}}{RT} = n_0\ln\alpha_0 + \sum_{i\neq 0} n_i\ln\!\left(\frac{\alpha_i}{\alpha_i^\infty}\right) \]
